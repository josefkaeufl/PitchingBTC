①Money has two jobs

  • To be good money, a thing must be a good asset (hold value) and a good network (move value).
  • Almost everything is good at one, not both — that’s the whole tension.
  • Set up the table: three contenders, two rows.

②Gold: asset, no network

  • A superb bearer asset — scarce, physical, no counterparty.
  • But its “network” is a plane: to get gold across the world, someone physically flies it there — slow and costly.
  • Move it at scale and you hand it to a custodian who gives you paper — now it’s not bearer anymore.

③The dollar: network, no asset

  • Worth knowing: paper money started life solving exactly this problem — a dollar bill was originally a claim you could redeem for gold in a vault, i.e. gold plus a better network on top.
  • The banking system is a global network — but permissioned, slow and full of middlemen.
  • And the asset is an IOU: you deposit it and hold a claim, not the thing itself.
  • 1971 (Nixon Shock): the dollar’s tie to gold was cut for good — the network stayed, the backing didn’t. The rules changed under everyone’s feet.

④Bitcoin does both · bridge

  • It’s a digital bearer asset and a native global network: final settlement in seconds, near-free, permissionless.
  • Mallers’ image: it’s like “faster gold” you can throw across the planet — and convert to any currency at each end.
  • Bridge: “So what is this asset actually worth — and why?”
