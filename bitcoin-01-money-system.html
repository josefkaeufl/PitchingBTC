Foundations · What is money?

Money is created from nothing.

Nobody asks you. No vote is taken. And most people have no idea who actually makes the dollars in their account.

  • The Fed creates new dollars out of thin air to buy bonds — no printing press required.
  • Your bank creates fresh money every time it makes a loan. It's a keystroke, not your neighbor's savings.
  • Repay the loan and that money vanishes again. Default, and it stays in the economy.
  • Cash is the exception. The overwhelming majority of dollars exist only as digital entries.

Speaker Guide How to present this slide · ~2–3 min

01Open with a question

  • Ask the room: "Where does a dollar actually come from?"
  • Most will say "the government prints it." Let that sit — then say: that's mostly wrong.
  • Hook: over 90% of dollars were never printed at all.

02The two money creators

  • The Fed creates base money digitally — e.g. buying bonds during QE.
  • Commercial banks create the rest by lending. Banks don't lend out savings — they create new deposits.
  • This isn't a conspiracy theory — it's how central banks describe it themselves.

03Use the loan analogy

  • "You take a $10,000 loan. The bank moves nothing from a vault — it just types 10,000 into your account."
  • That new money now exists and circulates in the economy.
  • Drive the kicker home: since 2020 the reserve requirement is 0% — nothing is legally required behind your deposit.

04Land it & transition

  • Takeaway: the supply of dollars is effectively unlimited, and you have no say in it.
  • Bridge to the next slide: "So what would money look like if it couldn't be created at will?"
  • That question is what Bitcoin answers.
