Foundations · What gives things value?

What makes anything valuable?

Scarce. Costly to make. Irreplaceable. Useful. Humans have always paid up for these traits — alone or in combination.

Where we already see it
In Bitcoin
Scarcefixed or limited supply

Gold, diamonds, your own time — nobody can make more of it on demand.

21 million cap, hard-coded and enforced by every node — no one can print more.

Costly to producelabor or energy intensive

Tons of ore moved to refine gold; a diamond cut from rock deep underground.

Real-world energy and hardware are spent to mine each block — its history is provably expensive to write.

Irreplaceableno substitute exists

An old photograph, a handwritten letter, a memory — the original or nothing.

Every coin's transaction history is unique and unforgeable — provenance you can't fake.

Usefulenables what you couldn't do before

A tool, a car, electricity — it extends what you're capable of.

Final, global settlement — move value anywhere, in seconds, without asking anyone's permission.

Value is rarely just one of these — it's usually a combination. Gold is scarce and costly to produce, but hard to move and easy to substitute. Bitcoin is the rare case where all four traits stack in a single asset.

Speaker Guide How to present this slide · ~3 min

①Scarce

  • Ask the audience: why is gold valuable and not, say, sand? Supply.
  • Bitcoin's 21M cap isn't a promise — it's enforced by every participant running the software. To change it, everyone would have to agree to devalue their own holdings. Nobody does.

②Costly to produce

  • Things that are hard to fake cheaply earn trust. That's why we don't value a photocopy of a painting.
  • Mining converts real energy into security: rewriting Bitcoin's history would cost more than any attacker could gain.

③Irreplaceable

  • This one is emotional, not economic — a childhood photo has no substitute at any price.
  • Every bitcoin carries its own full history back to creation — that provenance can't be copied onto a fake.

④Useful · bridge

  • Usefulness is what turns scarcity into demand — a scarce but useless thing stays a curiosity.
  • Bitcoin's usefulness is the network itself: final settlement anywhere, for anyone, without permission.
  • Bridge: "Most valuable things have one or two of these. Bitcoin is the first asset with all four at once — that's worth sitting with."
