Myths & objections · "Bitcoin wastes energy"

Energy, well spent.

Yes, it uses real energy. Here's what that energy replaces — and where it actually comes from.

Same order of magnitude, wildly different payoff: the supermarket estimate above is a transparent back-of-envelope calculation — full method in the speaker notes — but it shows Bitcoin's footprint isn't uniquely large, just uniquely visible and measurable, unlike the energy quietly baked into everyday retail. Cambridge puts 52% of mining on sustainable sources.

Speaker Guide How to present this slide · ~3–4 min

①Reframe, then actually rebut

  • "You've probably heard Bitcoin wastes huge amounts of energy."
  • Don't just deflect — give the comparison: everyday retail runs on a similar scale, invisibly.
  • The real question is what the energy buys and where it comes from — both halves matter.

②Walk the shelf diagram

  • One shelf, 30 near-identical varieties — this is one everyday habit among countless others we've stopped questioning.
  • Rough estimate: ~3.3 GWh/store/yr × 45,575 US supermarkets ≈ ~150 TWh/yr.
  • That's the same order of magnitude as Bitcoin's entire network — for variety almost nobody actually needs.
  • Right side: the same four traits of value from the "What makes anything valuable?" slide — scarce, costly to produce, irreplaceable, useful. Reuse that language if you've shown that slide already.

③Walk the "where it comes from" diagram

  • Three real sources: flared gas, curtailed wind & solar, and grid balancing.
  • All three are energy that would otherwise be burned off, switched off, or unused.
  • Proof in the wild: Riot Platforms, Texas, Aug 2023 — cut 95%+ load, earned $31.7M in grid payments.

④Land it with the sustainability number, bridge

  • 52% of Bitcoin mining now runs on sustainable sources (Cambridge, 2025) — above most national grids.
  • The point isn't "zero energy" — it's that Bitcoin's use is transparent and often literally otherwise-wasted.
  • Bridge: "The energy is real. So is what it replaces — and most of it was headed for waste anyway."

Supermarket estimate, step by step (rough, transparent, not peer-reviewed): ~42,000 sq ft average US store (FMI 2025) × ~51 kWh/sq ft/yr in-store electricity for lighting, refrigeration & HVAC (EIA/GreenChill) ≈ 2.1 GWh/yr. Plus logistics: ~8 truck deliveries/day (industry-cited average) × ~120 km round trip × ~35 L diesel/100km × ~10 kWh/L ≈ 1.2 GWh/yr. Total ≈ 3.3 GWh/store/yr; × 45,575 US supermarkets (FMI 2024) ≈ 150 TWh/yr. Treat this as an order-of-magnitude comparison, not an exact figure — and note it captures a store's whole energy use, not only the "excess variety" share of it. Bitcoin figure: Cambridge CBECI (~138 TWh) vs Digiconomist (~204 TWh) diverge by methodology; give the range. Gold (~240 TWh) and banking (~264 TWh) comparisons are Galaxy Digital, 2021, and predate Bitcoin's growth — cite for scale only. The Riot Texas case is one company, one event.
