Speaker Guide How to present this slide · ~4 min

①Frame it first

  • "Should I buy it?" is the question everyone has — and the honest answer is that it depends on your goals, horizon and risk tolerance. That is not mine to decide.
  • What I can do: show the numbers, show what a professional institution says, and mark clearly where my own view starts. The dark strip is opinion; the light cards are facts with sources.

②The numbers

  • Average growth per year, leaving out the early hype years: BTC ≈ $6.6k → $86k since Oct 2018 (≈ +38 % p.a.); ≈ $19.4k → $86k since Oct 2022 (≈ +45 % p.a.). US M2 over the same spans: ≈ +6.5 % and ≈ +2.2 % p.a. (FRED M2SL, Oct 2018 / Oct 2022 → Aug 2026).
  • Be upfront about the limits: the start date matters — Oct 2022 sits close to the late-2022 low (~$15.8k). An average hides the swings: BTC fell −53 % from its Oct 2025 high to its June 2026 low, and has since recovered to about $86k. Never extrapolate an average.
  • On M2: BlackRock notes that over the past decade bitcoin's returns have broadly tracked changes in the money supply and tended to do best when M2 growth accelerated — but that link weakened after the October 2025 sell-off. So "bitcoin more than kept up with money-supply growth" is accurate over these spans; "bitcoin tracks M2" is a looser claim.

③The professionals

  • The signal is that the world's largest asset manager publishes a dedicated report that treats bitcoin as a potential portfolio diversifier (low correlation, positively skewed returns, a hedge against fiat debasement).
  • Their 10-year analysis (May 2016 – May 2026): adding 1–2 % lifted the Sharpe ratio of a 60/40 portfolio from 0.81 to 0.90 / 0.96, with maximum drawdown barely changed (−20.3 % → −20.6 % / −20.9 %). It is hypothetical and backward-looking, and BlackRock itself says it is not a recommendation and that sizing depends on the investor.
  • Be fair to the audience: BlackRock sponsors a bitcoin ETF (IBIT), so it is not a neutral party. And it calls bitcoin volatile by any conventional standard — 40 % annualized vs 12 % for the S&P 500.

④My view — say it is a view

  • Price discovery: a 17-year-old asset that fell 53 % and rebounded within a year is still being priced. That cuts both ways.
  • AI as open internet money: a long-term thesis, not data. Note that BlackRock's own numbers show AI-themed funds drawing money away from bitcoin ETPs in 2025–26 — so the AI argument is about the future, not the recent past.
  • "No one can decree it": supply and protocol rules can't be changed by any single party — it takes broad consensus. Careful: that does not mean the price can't be pushed around. BlackRock describes leveraged futures and forced liquidations driving the sell-off.
  • Smartphone / head: access needs a phone and internet. A randomly generated 12–24-word recovery phrase can be memorized and restores the wallet on any compatible app — but forget it and the coins are gone; never invent your own phrase.
  • Close: "It's your decision. Understand it first."
