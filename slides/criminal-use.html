Myths & objections · "Bitcoin is for criminals"

Pseudonymous, not anonymous.

An address isn't an identity — until one link ties them together. From that moment on, the connection runs backward too.

Where it really bites: anyone who needs to stay hidden for years while remaining a prosecutable person is betting that no leak, ever, ties back to them — which is exactly what didn't hold for the 2016 Bitfinex hack: one gift card, six years later, exposed the entire trail retroactively. Illicit activity is still under 1% of on-chain volume (Chainalysis 2026) vs. 2–5% of global GDP laundered via cash & banks (UNODC) — though that gap owes as much to crypto's explosive legitimate growth and a shift toward mixers & stablecoins as to anyone being deterred outright.

Speaker Guide How to present this slide · ~3 min

①Fix the vocabulary first

  • "You've probably heard Bitcoin is a criminal's playground."
  • Correct one word: it's pseudonymous, not anonymous.
  • Card 1: the ledger — every amount, every timestamp — is public, forever.

②Show what's actually missing

  • Card 2: an address is just a string — no name attached by default.
  • That's the whole basis for "anonymous" — and it's thinner than people think.
  • It only takes one real-world link to close the gap.

③The link is the whole game

  • Card 3: KYC on an exchange, an IP address, reusing an address, one mistake.
  • Any single one is enough — and it works retroactively, not just going forward.
  • Unlike cash, nothing on the ledger can ever be edited or deleted.

④Be precise about the numbers, then bridge

  • The mechanism hits hardest for anyone needing to hide for years while staying prosecutable — proof in the wild: Bitfinex 2016 → 2022, one gift card, six years later, unraveled everything.
  • Under 1% of on-chain volume is illicit (Chainalysis) vs 2–5% of global GDP laundered via cash & banks (UNODC) — but don't oversell this as "criminals are too smart for it." A fast-growing legit market and a shift to mixers/stablecoins explain much of that gap too.
  • Bridge: "Cash and banks stay quiet. Bitcoin never forgets."

Note for the presenter: keep the mechanism as the headline. Don't claim the low illicit-share figure proves criminals broadly avoid Bitcoin out of caution — nation-state actors (largely unprosecutable) and fast-moving scammers use it heavily regardless, and the shrinking share partly reflects the legitimate market's growth. Chainalysis's figure is a lower-bound estimate; the UNODC range is cited for scale, not a like-for-like methodology match.
