Speaker Guide How to present this slide · ~2–3 min

01Read the chart out loud

  • "This is every dollar in the U.S. money supply since 1970."
  • Flat for decades, then a hockey stick — point at the 2020 COVID spike.
  • The headline: ~38× more dollars than in 1970.

02Run the $1,000 thought experiment

  • "Put $1,000 under the mattress in 1970."
  • Today it's about 1/38th of the money-supply share it had — like $26.
  • That's a 97% dilution of your slice of the pie.

03Pre-empt the obvious objection

  • Someone will say: "But I'd earn interest!" Agree — it's a fair point.
  • At ~4.7% T-bills, $1,000 → ~$12,500. Good… until you compare.
  • To merely keep your share of M2 you needed ~$38,000. Still −67%.

04Stay honest, then transition

  • Be fair: vs. grocery prices (CPI) the loss is smaller — this is about monetary debasement, your share of the money.
  • The point: you never agreed to be diluted, and you can't opt out.
  • Bridge: "Bitcoin is capped at 21 million. No one can vote to print more."

Note for the presenter: chart values are rounded annual approximations of FRED series M2SL; the loss figures are measured against M2 growth, not consumer prices.
