Gold and money. Two different sources.

Run both through the same four traits — and it turns out they get their value in almost opposite ways.

Gold earns its value from what it is. Money earns its value from what everyone agrees to do with it. Take away the mandate and the network — legal tender laws, taxes payable only in it, universal acceptance — and there's nothing left underneath.

Speaker Guide How to present this slide · ~3 min

①Recap the four traits

  • Quick callback to the previous slide: scarce, costly to produce, irreplaceable, useful.
  • "Let's run gold and money through the same checklist and see what happens."

②Gold: strong on the material side

  • Gold checks the first two boxes cleanly — that's why it's held value for millennia.
  • But it's fungible (one bar = another) and barely useful day-to-day — heavy, slow, no native network (callback to the asset/network slide).

③Money: zero for three, one by mandate

  • This is the punch: money checks none of the material traits — and your audience already knows why, from the "money is created from nothing" slide.
  • The only box it checks — useful — isn't a property of the money itself. It's a network effect: valuable purely because everyone else uses it too.
  • Add: legal tender laws force acceptance and force tax payments in it — demand is partly mandated, not chosen.

④Land it & bridge

  • Takeaway: gold's value rests on physics, money's value rests on trust and law — and both of those can shift under you (debasement, policy change, 1971).
  • Bridge: "What if something combined gold's physical scarcity with money's usefulness — without needing anyone's permission or trust to hold it together?"
  • That's the setup for Bitcoin.
