Game Theory.

Why not build our own digital money system? Bitcoin does — and this time, nobody can multiply the supply at will.

Decades of failed attempts, until this: Bitcoin is the first system to solve the Byzantine Generals Problem — strangers agreeing on one shared truth, with no referee. Adding a block costs real money and is rewarded; checking one is free — so honesty simply pays.

①What Bitcoin actually is

  • Not coins in a wallet — a shared record of every transaction ever made.
  • Add them all up and you get who owns what (the balances).
  • Everyone on the network keeps an identical copy — there is no central master.

②A block & why mining costs

  • Card 1: a block is just a bundle of transactions grouped together.
  • Card 2: to add one, a miner must burn real electricity solving proof of work.
  • That expense is the whole point — it puts real money at stake behind every block.

③Reward vs. penalty

  • Play fair → the block is accepted and the miner earns the block reward + fees.
  • Cheat or submit an invalid block → the electricity is spent for nothing and it's rejected.
  • So attacking literally costs more than it can pay. Honesty is the profitable strategy.

④The chain & the bridge

  • Card 3: each new block links to the one before it — one unbroken chain that only ever grows.
  • Changing an old transaction would break every block after it and mean re-mining them all — practically impossible.
  • Bridge: "It keeps growing — so what limits how many coins can ever exist?"
